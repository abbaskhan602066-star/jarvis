4.15.1 EGC Messages

EGC are a message broadcast service within the Inmarsat-C communication system. It al-
MES.

lows terrestrial information providers to pass messages or data to JUE-75A Inmarsat-
EGC mess

ges are sent to Land Earth Station by shore based Information Providers using
terrestrial facilities such as Telex and are processed at the LES, and forwarded to an NCS then
are broadcasted to the INMARSA-C MES via NCS common channel transmitted by NCS.

There are three basic services offered by EGC; the SafetyNET™ service, the FleetNET™
service and System service. SafetyNET™ is a service provided primarily for the dissemination
of maritime safety information, such as shore to ship distress alerts, weather forecasts and coastal
wamnings. FleetNET™ is a commercial communication service allowing terrestrial information
providers to send messages to pre-defined groups of subscribers. System service is a service
provided for operational information.

Both the SafetyNET™ and FleetNET™ services make use of a flexible addressing techniques
1o allow the reception of messages from a variety of service providers depending on the particu-
lar requirements of the user. The SafetyNET™ service utilizes a geographic area addressing
technique to direct messages to ships within defined boundary. The FleetNET™ service employs
closed user group and unique receiver addressing to provide secure transmission of messages
from the terrestrial information provider to the desired service recipient(s).

1. INTRODUCTION
11 Scope

Any person or entity desiring to operate an Inmarsat-C Ship Earth Station within the Inmarsat
system must have the ship earth station (SES) approved by Inmarsat for access to the system.
This approval is termed “Commissioning”. In order to obtain this approval certain information
must be supplied to Inmarsat and the ship earth station must undergo a series of automatic tests
to verify that it functions properly.

This document describes the procedures that the person or entity must follow in order to
have a ship earth station commissioned by Inmarsat. These procedures cover the information that
must be included in the application for a commissioning. The tests are completely automatic and
are invoked whenever a new MES attempts to make its first call.

The ship earth station licensee, however, is reminded that it is his responsibility to ensure
that any requirements with respect to licensing that are, or may be established by an entity
having jurisdiction over the operation of the ship earth station (generally the National Licensing
Authority) are met.

Any applicant fitting a Inmarsat-C ship earth station with the intention of satisfying the car-
riage requirements of the Global Maritime Distress and Safety System (GMDSS) should ensure
that the equipment is type approved as suitable for GMDSS fittings and that the installation
complies with Part A of the Inmarsat-C Design and Installation Guidelines. [Inmarsat-C System
Definition Manual Module 10].

The fact that these requirements have been met shall be confirmed by the applicant in the
Application for Commissioning. The ship will then be included in the list of Inmarsat GMDSS
equipped ships.

12 Type Approval Requirements
Only ship carth stations of models which have been type approved for use in the Inmarsat
system will be considered for commissioning. It will be the responsibility of the ship earth sta-
tion licensee to verify that the environmental requirements of the ship carth station model as
specified in the type approval certificate are compatible with the ship earth station installation.

3. PERFORMANCE VERIFICATION TEST (PVT)
3.1 General

The ship earth station equipment must be installed properly prior to the commissioning tests.
If the ship earth station equipment is intended to form part of the GMDSS, it must be installed
in accordance with Part A of the Inmarsat Design and Installation Guidelines. It is recommended
that the receiver should have been turned and synchronized to a NCS common channel carrier
for at least 20 minutes prior to initiating an automatic commissioning test.

32 Purpose of PVT

‘The PVT is a fully automatic test, requiring no actin from the operator and no measurement
equipment. It is designed to demonstrate that the ship earth station functions correctly. The sequence
of tests includes:

1) demonstration of satisfactory shore to ship message transfer and compliance with signal-
ling protocols;

2) measurement of the signal strength as received by the coast earth station is Inmarsat
made;

3) measurement of the SES’s received packet error rate;

4) a distress alert test.

4. APPROVAL FOR ACCESS

4.1 Successful Commissioning
Upon successful completion of the PVT, the SES may proceed to pass traffic.
42 Unsuccessful Commissioning

During the automatic testing sequence, each individual test routine may be performed up to
three times. Additionally, the operator is free to attempt a further two re-tests if the first test is
failed. If the equipment fails the first or second attempt, the operator is advised to investigate the
causes of failure.

After the third attempt, if a failure is still registered, the SES will not be permitted to pass
traffic other than distress alerting.

4.3 Duration

Approval of a particular ship earth station installation for access to the Inmarsat system will

remain valid until any one of the following occurs:

(a) removal of the complete ship earth station equipment; or

(b) any change in the information contained in the commissioning application which would
require a change in the ship station identity; or

(c) persistent malfunction or any operation of the ship earth station that degrades the per-
formance of the Inmarsat system.

Approval for access to the Inmarsat system is given to a particular ship installation as it
exists at the time of commissioning.

Therefore, if (a) occurs and the ship earth station equipment is installed on another ship, a
new commissioning application must be submitted and the ship earth station recommissioned for
use on the new ship. If (b) or () occurs, the Inmarsat OCC must be promptly notified.

The SES operator may request a PVT at any time. In particular, following equipment repair
or replacements, it is recommended that he should do so before attempting communication.

L. Introduction

‘Welcome to the world of mobile satellite communications.

The Inmarsat-C system, in which this mobile earth station is designed to operate, was set up
to provide low cost message-based telex and data communications to mobile users the world
over utilising the high integrity Inmarsat system of satellites and ground earth stations. Com-
munications via the Inmarsat system provides users with unlimited access to and form the glo-
bal telecommunication networks from virtually anywhere in the world 24 hours a day, all year
round.

The global coverage capability and high reliability features of satellite communications have
prompted the International Maritime Organization (IMO) to adopt the Inmarsat-C system as a
primary means of Safety of Life at Sea communications equipment or all vessels over 300 grt
sailing in waters beyond the coverage of conventional radio systems. Small vessels under 300
grt may also take advantage of the distress and safety related facilities of the Inmarsat-C system.

Inmarsat provides world-wide satellite communications to all mobile users, whether on land,
sea or in the air. Under certain conditions, communications may also be provided to fixed
locations.

2. Who or What is Inmarsat?

Inmarsat, the international co-operative established in 1979 for maritime mobile satellite
communications and in operation since 1982, has since evolved into the space-segment and sys-
tem provider for a complete, comprehensive and worldwide set of mobile satellite services for all
mobile user communities-maritime, aeronautiacal and land.

Since the Inmarsat has expanded its operations to include the provision of mobile satellite
communication services to users in the aeronautical and land mobile environment.

With almost 60 member countries, Inmarsat is a unique example of cooperation between
nations and is dedicated to providing the benefits of satellite communications to all.

4. What is commissioning?

In order of you to gain access to the system, it is necessary for your mobile earth station to
be commissioned. During commissioning the equipment needs to undergo a sequence of simple
tests to demonstrate that it functions correctly and is capable of providing reliable communica-
tions without jeopardising the integrity of the Inmarsat system. In addition, for an Inmarsat-C
mobile earth station intended to meet the requirements of the Global Maritime Distress and
Safety System, commissioning demonstrates that the installed configuration of equipment is a
reliable element of the Distress and Safety System.

Before commissioning tests can take place, information is required concerning the equip-
ment, the owner/licensee, the billing authority and your local Routing Organization, to whom the
completed application is sent. In addition you will be allocated an Inmarsat Mobile Number,
which will used by subscribers to terrestrial services to communicate with you.

INMARSAT-C NUMBERING PLAN
SHIP STATION IDENTITY

The Ship Station Identity which is issued by the Routing Organization, is defined in CCIR
Radio Regulations, Appendix 43, as a 9 digit code in the following form:

Ship Station Identity = M1 12 D3 X4 X5 X6 X7 X8 X9

‘The three digit MID represents the Maritime Identification Digits, one or more being allo-
cated to each country.

X may have any value from 0 through 9.

Due to restrictions on national switching equipment, X9 cannot be used at present.

Digits X7 X8 are used for discrimination of MESs and instruments attached to MESs on
ships are denoted by Z1 and Z2, i.e.:

M ID X1 X2 X3 Z1 22

THE INMARSAT MOBILE NUMBER

‘The Inmarsat Mobile Number is derived from the Ship Station Identity and is the Interna-
tional number used by callers wishing to call the Mobile Earth Station via the Public Switched
Networks.

The Inmarsat Mobile Number takes the Ship Station Identity and prefixes it with a special
digit (the T digit) which is used to signify to which inmarsat sytem (Inmarsat-A, Inmarsat-B etc),
the terminal is working through. The inmarsat-C T digit is 4.

For land mobile earth stations the T digit, r, is followed by a 9 with the option to replace it
with a 9 at Inmarsat’s discretion (see CCITT recommendation E.215). The maritime MID is also
replaced by a Mobile Country Code, which is also assigned by CCITT.

« The Inmarsat Mobile Number for ship earth stations shall therefore be of the format:
4MID X1 X2X32Z17Z2
