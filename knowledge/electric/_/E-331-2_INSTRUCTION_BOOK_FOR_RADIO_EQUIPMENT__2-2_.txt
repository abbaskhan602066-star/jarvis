1.0 Iatroduction

The IQE-2A-E 406MHz Satellite Emergency Position Indicating Radio Beacon (Satcllite
EPIRB) is designed for use by marimers in distress to initiate the transmission of an
emergency distress locating signal to the COSPAS/SARSAT Sateilites.

The IQE-24-E EPIRB houses two (2) tramsmitters. When the JQE-24-E is activated,
one tramsmitter transmits the digitally encoded emergency message which contains the
vessels identity and nationality to the satellite at a frequency of 404.025MHz

When the 406.025YHz emergency signal is detected by the satellits, it is relayed
to a ground receiving station which can quickly determine the location of the vessel
in disiress, anyshere in the vorld. vithir a few niles

The location of the vessel in distress is immedialely relzved through a Mission
Control Center to an Aviation or Maritime Rescue Coordinaties Center in the vieinity
of the vessel, so that Search and Rescue units can be deployed immediately.

The second transmitter transmils a signal on 121.5MHz which is used by search and
rescue units as a "homing” signal. There is also a strobe light built inte the top of
the JQE-24-E shich upon activation will begin to flash. providing a visual aid to the
search and rescue units.

The global coverage provided by Lhe COSPAS/SARSAT satellites greatly increases the
chance of hearing the distress signals anywhere in the world. The basic system iz shown
in Fig. 1-1.

The JQE-2A-E is capable of transmitting the t¥o emergency signals and the visual
indication for a minimum of 48 hours from the time of activation and at temperalure
extremes of -40 to +55 degrees C.

The JQE-2A-E EPIRB with a Float Free Release Bracket meets the requirements of the
ETSI (Buropean Telecommunications Standards Institute) class 1.

The JQE-ZA-E's rugged design and waterproof construction meet all the requirements
of IMO/CCIR, ETSI, and COSPAS/SARSAT to emsure reliable operation in the marine
environment.
