INTRODUCTION

The Sasakura Super Trident Sewage Treatment Plant is manufactured under the
technical licence agreement with Hamworthy Engineering Ltd., U.K.

The Sasakura Super Trident Sewage Treatment Plant is a system for the complete
purification and treatment of sewage from ships and rigs before it is discharged
overboard and thus prevents the pollution of harbours, coastal waters, and inland
waterways.

This system approved by the U.S. Coast Guard as the "Type Il Marine Sanitation
Device" and also certified by DOT, England, based on the international Standard, IMO
(1973), can efficiently cope with the warld's existing and prospected sewage discharge
control regulations.

The system adopts the activated sludge process, especially total oxidation (extended
aeration) process to minimize excess sludge. Also, the system is provided with a
sterilization means for the treated sewage. Thus, the system is characterized as a
very compact, highly efficient sewage treatment plant and can be used satisfactory
on salt, fresh or brackish waters.

Sasakura Super Trident has ST-2A - ST-6A models equipped with exclusive air
compressar on the skid, and ST-2AN - ST-6AN models exclusive compressor
eliminated by using ships' compressed air. This manual describes for

5T-2AN - ST-6AN.

GENERAL

This system adopts activated sludge process which is a water purification process
utilizing the biological sludge called activated sludge. Biological sludge is of
complicated components, mainly consists of aerobic bacteria to which adheres
suspended solid both organic and inorganic, including protozoa, metazoa, and bacillus.
These aerobic bacteria are highly adsorptive and are able to break down organisms by
combustion activities of these microbes. When placed static, these aerobic bacteria
flocculate, forming flocs which facilitate settiing out of themselves. While alr is
supplied 1o the sewage by aerating, the sewage and activated sludge contact each
other.

Consequently, activated sludge adsorbs most of the waste matters in the sludge both
organic and inorganic with its powerful adsorbing ability.

The adsorbed waste matters serve as nutritions source for microbes in the sludge.
Part of the adsorbed waste matters is broken down into such harmless elements as

water, carbon dioxide, etc, Part of it forms cell of the activated sludge.

--- PAGE 22 ---
3-1

Various ways can be considered as the activated sludge process depending on each
purpose. One of them is a method usually called "Complete Oxidation Process" which
is adopted by Sasakura Super Trident Sewage Treatment Plant.

In the complete oxidation process, the sewage and the activated sludge are contacted
each other for a very long hours, with which waste matters (nutritions source for the
activated sludge) per sludge unit are relatively decreased and microbes are
consequently starved and begin metabolic activities using their own cytoplasm. Thus
they consume most of their own cytoplasm which they once synthesized. And so,
cven after an operation of long hours, quantity of the sludge increase is extremely
small, which is desirable for an easy maintenance and is the most suitable method for
a compact plant. Immediately after the satisfactory contact of the activated sludge
with the sewage and holding them still, the activated sludge forms large flocs and
casily settles out. The bacteria settled out as the activated sludge are returned to
the aeration compartment in order to maintain the constant sludge density in the

aeration compartment.

General Description

The Sasakura Super Trident Sewage Treatment Plant comprises three compartments-
an aeration compartment, a clarification compartment, and a chlorination
compartment, as shown in Fig.l. The incoming sewage enters the aeration
compartment, where it is supplied with air and is digested and broken down by the
activated sludge. The sewage, after being fully purified in the aeration
compartment, flows into the clarification compartment, where the activated sludge
settles out and the pure clean water separated fram the activated sludge is sterilized

in the chlorination compartment and then is discharged overboard.

Aeration Compartment

In this compartment of the treatment plants, the activated sludge which imbibes
dissolved oxygen reduces the influent waste material which mainly comprises Carbon,
Oxygen, Hydrogen, Nitrogen, and Sulphur into Carbon Dioxide, water and new
bacteria cells.

The carbon dioxide is emitted through the vent system, while the water together with
the bacteria cells are displaced, after approximately 24 hours, into the clarification
compartment. Air is supplied to the sewage by connecting with ship's compressed air
system through a number of fine bubble diffusers, located at the bottom of the tank
but removable from a manhole at its side for ease of maintenance. This air provides
the life giving oxygen to the aerabic organisms and also keeps the contents of the
tank intimately mixed with the incoming raw sewage and the returned settled

activated sludge.

Clarification Compartment

The mixed fluid flowed into the clarification compartment settles out and the pure
clean water separated from the activated sludge is sent to the chlorination
compartment through an over-flow pipe located in the clarification compartment,
while the activated sludge settled out is returned to the aeration compartment from
the bottom of the clarification compartment by the airlift tube, via a visual vinyl
hose which enables a check to be made on the sludge return.

The clarification compartment is of the hopper type. The sloping sides prevent the
settled sludge from accumulating and direct it to the suction side of the air lift tube.
A skimmer is provided at the upper center of the clarification compartment to skim
off and return surface debris back to the aeration tank by way of the same method
taken for the return of the activated sludge.

Chlorination Compartment

The pure clean water overflowed from the clarification compartment dissolves
chiorination tablets in the chlorinator and then flows into the chlorination
compartment, where it is sterilized.

At the side of the tank provided are two level regulators {float switch) to control the
operation of pump, An additional float switch is fitted which operates an alarm
signal should the level rise above the normal "high” level position. An emergency
overfiow pipe is fitted to prevent abnormal overflow of the treated water to the

outside of the tank.

Chlorinator

In the chlorinatar the pure clean water passes through sterilizing tablets in such a
way that it directly contacts the tablets, thus chlorine solution is made. The
chlorinator comprises rectangular chlorinator body and three cylinders for filling the
chlorinator with tablets.

At the bottom of the filling cylinder several cuts are provided to bring the flowed-in
fluid contact with tablets placed in the cylinder, and the required amount of chlorine
is dissolved. At the outlet of the chlorinator is a control weir arranged so that as the
effluent flow increases the level rises and more tablets come into contact with the
effluent. In this way the chlorine uptake is always sufficient for sterilization of the
efiluent.

Usually, the pump automatically discharges the treated water during operation and
with a manual operation taking out the sludge from each compartment is enabled
during the cleaning of the tank inside,

For details of the pump, please refer to the separate Instruction Manual.

5. REMARKS

5.1 Remarks on Operation

At the operation of this system in which the sewage is treated by the biological

sludge, the followings ought to be obeyed.

(1} After the starting up of the system, it takes a certain period (usually from 10
days to one month, though can be varied depending upon the operating
circumstancies) before the aercbic bacteria breed.

Therefore, the expected result can not always be obtained from the very
beginning. As bacteria get breeded, the treated water improves its quality sa
the operation have to be continued without suspension.

(This fact is true of the re-operation after the Cleaning.)

{2) Even outside the restricted waters, the system shall be kept operated, where,
however, chlorination is not necessary. If the operation is suspended for more
than 24 hours, treatment efficiency would be affected due to decreased or died

out bacteria caused by lack of oxygen and nutrition supply to aerobic bacteria.

{3} If the system has to be suspended by any reason, be sure to clean the inside fully.
For the method of cleaning, refer to "6-2 Cleaning".

{4) Ship's compressed air must be supplied to the plant so as to keep the treatment
efficiency normally.
When ship's air should not be available by some reason, the system shall not be
used.

(5) It is recommended not ta use chemicals for cleaning the lavatory which if mixed

into, might destroy the aerobic bacteria and deteriorate the system,

(6) If the electricity stoppage continues long, prohibit the use of the lavatory or

take a necessary measure to block entering of the sewage into the system.

(7) Provide a litter box in the lavatory to avoid those materials as bits of cloth,

paper towel or viny! being thrown away into the lavatory.
{8) If the flushing water of stools is kept flowing, the water flow into the system

becomes excessive and the treatment efficiency would be affected. Be sure to

flush water only when the lavatory is used and to stop flushing after use.

INSTALLATION

Install the pump lower than the suction water level to ensure forced
operation.

The pump bed shall be strong enough to bear the weight of the pump and
others, and the bed shall be horizontal.

Don't put the weight of the piping on the pump. If any load is put on the
pump, adequate supports must be provided.

Note that any excessive load onto the pump deforms the pump body, makes
the lives of bearings and mechanical seals short, and causes leakage from

mechanical seals.

HANDLING
Inspection before initial operation

Prior to the initial operation after completion of installation or re-

assembling, following points shall be checked.

(1) Remove the dust-proof cloth tape from the penetrated part of the
pump shaft. (Be careful not to put any foreign things into the gap in
the shaft.)

{2)  Turn the coupling by hand two to three times to check smooth
revolution of it.

(3)  Contirm rotating direction of the motor. If wrong, correct wiring.

(4)  Make sure that the valve on the suction side piping is fully opened.

Starting-up -

(1) Close the discharge valve fully.

(2)  Open the suction valve fully.

{3)  Start the pump.

(4)  Qpen the discharge valve gradually.

Stopping

(1) Close the discharge valve first and then turn off the switch.

(2)  In case the pump is dismounted from the piping for storage, screw off
the drain plug to discharge water in the casing.

(3) I the pump which has already been piped like a stand-by pump is not
used for a long time, it must be tentatively operated for about 5

minutes at least once a month.
